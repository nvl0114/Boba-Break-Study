Basic Sentence: Subject + Verb + Object

Learn how to say what people do, make it negative, and ask yes/no questions.

🧠 Grammar Pattern

A basic Chinese sentence is built like an English sentence: who (Subject, S) + does what (Verb, V) + to what (Object, O). Chinese verbs do not change form, so 吃 stays 吃 for I, you, he, she, we, and they.

Positive (+)
S+V+O

Put the subject first, then the verb, then the object.

Negative (−)
S+不+V+O

Put 不 (bù) right before the verb. It means “do not / does not”.

Questions (?) — Two ways

Way 1: Add 嗎 (ma) at the end.

S+V+O+嗎？

嗎 is a question particle. It turns a normal sentence into a yes/no question.

Way 2: Use V + 不 + V.

S+V+不+V+O?

Say the verb, then 不, then the verb again: “eat or not eat?”. It also makes a yes/no question.

Remember!
Use either 嗎 or V + 不 + V — do not use both in the same question.
✓ 你吃飯嗎？
✓ 你吃不吃飯？
✗ 你吃不吃飯嗎？
How to answer yes/no questions

In Chinese, we usually answer by repeating the verb. You do not need to say 是 here, because 是 is used for identity (like “I am a student”).

Yes = the verb
e.g. 吃 (chī)
No = 不 + the verb
e.g. 不吃 (bù chī)
QUESTION (嗎)
你吃飯嗎？
Nǐ chī fàn ma?
Do you eat?
ANSWER (YES)
吃！
Chī!
Yes, I do. (literally: eat)
QUESTION (V不V)
你吃不吃飯？
Nǐ chī bu chī fàn?
Do you eat?
ANSWER (NO)
不吃！
Bù chī!
No, I don't. (literally: not eat)
Tip: The answers are the same for both question types (嗎 and V不V).

Compare the four sentence forms

Listen to each sentence and notice how the meaning changes.
