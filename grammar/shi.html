GRAMMAR GAME • IDENTITY

是 (shì): Am, Is, Are

Learn how to say who someone is, what they do, and where they are from.

🧠 Grammar Pattern

是 (shì) is similar to am / is / are in English. Use it to connect the subject to a noun or noun phrase that tells us who or what someone is.

Positive (+)
Subject+是+Noun / identity

Use 是 to make a positive sentence.

我是學生
Wǒ shì xuéshēng
I am a student.
Negative (−)
Subject+不是+Noun / identity

Put 不 before 是 to make the sentence negative. 不是 means “am not / is not / are not”.

我不是學生
Wǒ bú shì xuéshēng
I am not a student.
Questions (?) — Two ways

Way 1: Add 嗎 (ma) at the end.

Subject+是+Noun / identity+嗎？

嗎 is a question particle. It goes at the very end of the sentence to turn it into a yes/no question.

你是學生嗎？
Nǐ shì xuéshēng ma?
Are you a student?

Way 2: Use 是不是.

Subject+是不是+Noun / identity?

是不是 literally repeats the positive and negative forms: “是 or not 是”. It also makes a yes/no question.

你是不是學生？
Nǐ shì bú shì xuéshēng?
Are you a student?
Remember!
Use either 嗎 or 是不是 — do not use both in the same question.
✓ 你是學生嗎？
✓ 你是不是學生？
✗ 你是不是學生嗎？
Asking someone's nationality: Subject + 是 + 哪國人

Use 哪國人 to ask which country someone is from.

哪 = which · 國 = country · 人 = person

Because 哪 already makes the sentence a question, you do not need 嗎 or 是不是.

你是哪國人？
Nǐ shì nǎ guó rén?
Which country are you from?
我是台灣人
Wǒ shì Táiwān rén
I am Taiwanese.
Tip: In this lesson, 是 is followed by a noun or identity phrase, such as 學生, 老師, 員工, or 印尼人. It is not normally used directly before an adjective such as 忙 or 漂亮.

Compare the four sentence forms

Listen to each sentence and notice how the meaning changes.

我是大學生
Wǒ shì dàxuéshēng
I am a university student.
我不是大學生
Wǒ bú shì dàxuéshēng
I am not a university student.
我是不是大學生？
Wǒ shì bú shì dàxuéshēng?
Am I a university student?
我是大學生嗎？
Wǒ shì dàxuéshēng ma?
Am I a university student?
