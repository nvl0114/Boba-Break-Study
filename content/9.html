🧠 Grammar Notes & Quick Patterns

1. 塊 vs 元 — Money Units

元 (yuán) vs 塊 (kuài)

Explanation: 元 is the formal currency unit ("dollar"), while 塊 is the casual spoken way to say "dollar". In Taiwan, 塊 is very common in daily conversation.

Examples: 100 元 = 100 塊 (100 dollars)

這個一百塊。
Zhège yì bǎi kuài.
This one is 100 dollars.

2. 給 (gěi) — Give / For

Subject + 給 + Person + Object

Explanation: Used to indicate giving something to someone, or meaning "for".

Example: 這是給你的。(This is for you.)

我給你錢。
Wǒ gěi nǐ qián.
I give you money.

3. 不好意思 vs 對不起

Polite Apology & Attention

Explanation: 不好意思 is used for small inconveniences, getting attention, or being polite (very common in Taiwan). 對不起 is more direct and used when you did something wrong.

不好意思，請問有沒有茶？
Bù hǎoyìsi, qǐngwèn yǒu méiyǒu chá?
Excuse me, do you have tea?

4. 不客氣 vs 不會

Responding to 謝謝 (Thank you)

Explanation: 不客氣 is standard textbook "You're welcome", while 不會 is commonly heard from locals in Taiwan meaning "No problem / Don't mention it".

不客氣 / 不會
Bú kèqì / Bú huì
You're welcome / No problem.

5. 完 (wán) + 了 — Completed Actions

V + 完了

Explanation: Indicates that an action has been finished or completed (e.g., 賣完了 sold out, 吃完了 finished eating).

茶賣完了。
Chá mài wán le.
The tea is sold out.

6. 沒關係 vs 沒問題

It's okay vs No problem

Explanation: 沒關係 (It's okay / No worries) is often used after 對不起. 沒問題 (No problem) is used when accepting a request.

沒關係。
Méi guānxi.
That's okay / No worries.
