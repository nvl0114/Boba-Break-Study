🧠 Grammar & Sentence Explanations

1. 塊 vs 元 — Money Units

元 (yuán) vs 塊 (kuài):

元 is the formal currency unit ("dollar"), while 塊 is the casual spoken way to say "dollar". In Taiwan, 塊 is very common in daily conversation.

💡 Example: 100 元 = 100 塊 (100 dollars)

2. 給 (gěi) — "Give / To / For"

Basic Pattern: Subject + 給 + Person + Object

Used for giving items or doing something for someone, or meaning "for".

💡 Example 1: 我給你錢。 (I give you money.)
💡 Example 2: 這是給你的。 (This is for you.)

3. 不好意思 vs 對不起

Polite apologies and excuses:

不好意思 is much more common in everyday Taiwan Mandarin for small inconveniences, getting attention, or being polite. 對不起 is more direct and used when you did something wrong.

💡 Example: 不好意思，請問... (Excuse me, may I ask...)

4. 不客氣 vs 不會

Responding to 謝謝 (Thank you):

不客氣 is the standard textbook expression ("You're welcome"), while 不會 is also commonly heard from locals in Taiwan ("No problem / Don't mention it").

5. 完 (wán) + 了 — "Finished / Completed"

V + 完了 pattern:

Indicates that an action has been finished or completed.

💡 Example: 蘋果賣完了。 (The apples are sold out.)

6. 沒關係 vs 沒問題

It's okay vs No problem:

沒關係 means "It's okay / No worries" (often used after 對不起). 沒問題 means "No problem" (used when accepting a request or confirming something is fine).

💡 Example: A: 對不起！ B: 沒關係！
