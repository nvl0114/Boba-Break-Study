Numbers & Money

Learn numbers beyond 10, large scale counting units, and currency expressions in Chinese.

📖 Lesson Breakdown

We have already learned numbers 1-10 and teens in previous lessons. Let's learn more:

11–19

十 (shí) + number

Example: 十二 (shí èr = 12), 十八 (shí bā = 18)

20–99

number + 十 (shí) + number

Example: 二十 (èr shí = 20), 八十五 (bā shí wǔ = 85)

Hundred (百 bǎi)

Example:

兩百 (liǎng bǎi = 200)

五百三十五 (wǔ bǎi sān shí wǔ = 535)

Notice this: 五百 (wǔ bǎi = 500), 三十 (sān shí = 30), 五 (wǔ = 5)

Thousand (千 qiān)

Example:

兩千 (liǎng qiān = 2000)

八千三百五十六 (bā qiān sān bǎi wǔ shí liù = 8356)

Notice this: 八千 (bā qiān = 8000) + 三百 (sān bǎi = 300) + 五十 (wǔ shí = 50) + 六 (liù = 6)

Tens of Thousands (萬 wàn)

In Chinese culture, numbers are not grouped by thousands (groups of 3 zeros) like in the West, but rather grouped in units of 4 zeros.

Explanation:

  • When looking at a number like 10000 (4 zeros after 10), we group it as 10.000. For 1000000 (6 zeros), we split it as 1.000.000.
  • In Chinese culture, they have a dedicated unit for 4 zeros, which is 萬 (wàn).

Examples:

20000 = 2,0000 = 兩萬 (liǎng wàn)

21,517 = 二萬一千五百一十七 (èr wàn yī qiān wǔ bǎi yī shí qī)

  • 兩萬 (èr wàn): 2,0000
  • 一千 (yī qiān): 1,000
  • 五百 (wǔ bǎi): 500
  • 一十 (yī shí): 10 (Here 一十 appears because it sits right after 五百: 五百 + 一十 + 七 = 500 + 10 + 7. However, if standing alone, 10 is usually just 十, not 一十)
  • 七 (qī): 7

For 1 Million

1000000 = 100,0000 = 一百萬 (yī bǎi wàn)

  • 一百 (yī bǎi): 100
  • 萬 (wàn): 1,0000

Currency Units

塊 (kuài): Colloquial measure word for currency units (like "bucks" or "dollars").

元 (yuán): Formal written unit for currency (RMB / Yuan / Dollars).

臺幣 / 台幣 (táibì): New Taiwan Dollar (NTD).
