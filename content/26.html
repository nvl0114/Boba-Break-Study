🧠 Grammar Patterns: Talking About Time, Actions & Feelings

1. 才 (cái) — "Not Until / Only Then"

Subject + Time / Condition + 才 + Verb

才 is used when something happens later than expected, or only after a certain time, condition, or situation is reached. It often carries the meaning of "not until...", "only then...", or "only finally...".

The important idea is not simply time. 才 highlights a delay or the fact that an action could not happen until that point.

For example: 我十點才到家。 means "I didn't get home until 10." The speaker arrived at 10, but 10 is later than expected.

In the conversation: 為什麼今天晚上才寫呢？ means "Why did you only get around to writing it tonight?" The point is that the homework was done later than expected.

我十點才到家。
Wǒ shí diǎn cái dào jiā.
I didn't get home until 10.

2. 還沒 (hái méi) — "Not Yet"

Subject + 還沒 + Verb

還沒 means that something has not happened yet at the present moment.

還 means "still," while 沒 negates a completed action. Together, 還沒 expresses "still haven't..." or "not yet."

It is commonly used when you expect the action to happen later. It is different from simply saying 不 + Verb, which often expresses a general refusal, habit, or future decision.

In the conversation: 你為什麼還沒睡？ means "Why haven't you slept yet?" The speaker expected the person to be asleep already.

我還沒吃飯。
Wǒ hái méi chīfàn.
I haven't eaten yet.

3. V + 著 (zháo) — Result of an Action

Verb + 著 (zháo)

In words such as 睡著, 著 is a resultative complement. It tells us that the action reaches a particular result or state.

For example: 睡 means "to sleep," but 睡著 means "to fall asleep". The result of the action is that the person successfully enters a sleeping state.

The same type of meaning appears in some other expressions. For example, 找著 can mean "successfully find" in appropriate contexts.

Important: 著 has more than one pronunciation and grammatical function. Here, in 睡著, it is pronounced zháo and shows the result of the action.

This is different from V + 著 (zhe), which can describe a continuing state. For example: 門開著。 means "The door is open." Here, 著 is pronounced zhe and describes an ongoing state.

In the conversation: 結果後來睡著了。 means "As a result, I fell asleep afterward."

我終於睡著了。
Wǒ zhōngyú shuìzháo le.
I finally fell asleep.

4. 不用 (búyòng) — "Do Not Need To"

不用 + Verb / Verb Phrase

不用 can be understood as 不 + 用. Here, 用 means "to need" or "to have to" in this expression. So 不用 means "do not need to..." or "do not have to...".

That is why 不用 is normally followed by an action: 不用擔心 = "You don't need to worry," 不用客氣 = "You don't need to be so polite," and 不用等 = "You don't need to wait."

不用 is not the same as 不要. 不要 + Verb usually tells someone not to do something, while 不用 + Verb says that doing it is unnecessary.

For example: 不要擔心。 = "Don't worry." 不用擔心。 = "You don't need to worry."

In the conversation: 你不用這麼緊張。 means "You don't need to be so nervous." It reassures the listener rather than telling them what they must not do.

你不用擔心。
Nǐ búyòng dānxīn.
You don't need to worry.

5. 這麼 (zhème) — "So / This Much"

這麼 + Adjective / Adverb

這麼 refers to a degree or amount that feels this high, this much, or so... It is often used when the speaker is emphasizing the degree of something.

With an adjective, it can be translated as "so...", "this...", or "this much...", depending on the context.

For example: 這麼緊張 means "so nervous" or "this nervous," while 這麼晚 means "this late."

It can also refer to actions or amounts. For example: 你怎麼這麼早來？ means "Why did you come so early?"

In the conversation: 你不用這麼緊張啦！ means "You don't need to be so nervous!"

今天怎麼這麼冷？
Jīntiān zěnme zhème lěng?
Why is it so cold today?

6. 以為 (yǐwéi) — "To Think, but Be Wrong"

Subject + 以為 + Clause

以為 means "to think" or "to believe," but it normally implies that the belief is different from reality. In other words, the speaker believed something, but later discovered that it was not true.

This makes 以為 different from 覺得, which simply expresses someone's opinion, feeling, or impression.

For example: 我以為你不來了。 means "I thought you weren't coming," and usually suggests that you actually came.

In the conversation: 你以為我們只是好朋友嗎？ literally asks, "Do you think we're just good friends?" The question challenges what Xiaomei believes about their relationship.

我以為你不來了。
Wǒ yǐwéi nǐ bù lái le.
I thought you weren't coming. (But you came.)

7. 結果 / 後來 — "As a Result / Later"

Event 1 → 結果 + Outcome → 後來 + Later Event

結果 introduces the result or outcome of a previous event. It is often used when the outcome is unexpected or different from what was planned.

後來 means "later," "afterward," or "later on." It moves the story forward and tells us what happened afterward.

The two words have different functions: 結果 focuses on the outcome, while 後來 focuses on what happened later.

In the conversation: 我昨天一直在想要送你什麼禮物，結果後來睡著了。 means that Ahao was thinking about the gift, and the unexpected result was that he eventually fell asleep.

我一直在等他，結果後來睡著了。
Wǒ yìzhí zài děng tā, jiéguǒ hòulái shuìzháo le.
I kept waiting for him, and eventually I fell asleep.
