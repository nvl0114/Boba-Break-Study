🧠 Grammar & Sentence Explanations

1. Asking Names, Surnames & Polite Inquiries

叫 (jiào) & 什麼名字 (shénme míngzi):

Used to ask or state someone's given name using the pattern: Name + 叫 + Name.

💡 Example: 您叫什麼名字？ (What is your name?)
貴姓 (guìxìng):

A polite, formal way to ask someone's surname (literally "honorable surname"). Respond with 姓 + Surname.

💡 Example: 您貴姓？ → 我姓林。 (What is your surname? → My surname is Lin.)

2. Age Expression (幾歲 & 歲)

幾 (jǐ):

An interrogative word meaning "how many" (typically used for numbers under 10).

💡 Example: 您幾歲？ (How old are you?)
歲 (suì):

Measures years of age. Placed directly after the age number following the pattern: Number + 歲.

💡 Example: 我二十歲。 (I am 20 years old.)

3. Tens Formation in Numbers

Tens structure:

Tens are constructed by placing a multiplier digit before 十 (shí) and any remainder unit after it.

💡 Example 1: 二十 (èrshí) = 2 × 10 = 20.
💡 Example 2: 二十二 (èrshí'èr) = 20 + 2 = 22.

4. Topic Marker / Turn-Tossing (呢)

呢 (ne):

Placed at the end of a noun or pronoun to bounce a question back to the conversation partner (similar to "How about...?" or "And you?").

💡 Example: 我叫美美。您呢？ (My name is Meimei. How about you?)

5. Interrogative Questions with 什麼

什麼 (shénme - what):

Unlike English, question words in Mandarin stay in the exact same position where the answer goes in a regular sentence statement.

💡 Statement: 我的愛好是電影。
💡 Question: 您的愛好是什麼？ (What is your hobby?)

6. Expressing "Also / Too" (也)

也 (yě):

Means "also" or "too". Rule of word order: 也 always goes immediately before the verb or adjective, right after the subject.

💡 Example: 我也很高興認識您！ (I *also* am very glad to meet you!)
