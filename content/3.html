The Verb 是 (Shì) & Questions

Learn how to connect identities, state facts, and answer questions.

🧠 Grammar Patterns: 是 (Shì)

1. Affirmative Identity: 是 (Shì)

Use 是 (shì) like "to be" (am/is/are) to connect a subject to a noun/identity.

Subject
+
是
shì
+
Noun

是 (shì) equates the subject to the noun phrase that follows it.

我 是 醫生
Wǒ shì yīshēng.
I am a doctor.

2. Negative Identity: 不是 (Bú shì)

Place 不 (bù) before 是 to say "is not / am not / are not".

Subject
+
不是
bú shì
+
Noun

Note: Due to tone sandhi rules, 不 (bù) changes to bú when followed by a 4th tone word like 是 (shì).

他 不 是 老闆
Tā bú shì lǎobǎn.
He is not the boss.

3. Question Particle: 是 ... 嗎 (Shì ... ma)

Turn any 是 sentence into a yes/no question by putting 是 before the noun and 嗎 at the end.

是
shì
...
嗎
ma

Structure: Subject + 是 + Noun + 嗎？

你 是 學生 嗎
Nǐ shì xuésheng ma?
Are you a student?

4. Alternative Question: 是不是 (Shì bú shì)

Ask "is it or isn't it?" using 是不是 right before the noun or predicate.

Subject
+
是不是
shì bú shì
+
Noun

This prompts confirmation from the listener (similar to "Right?" or "Isn't it true that...").

她 是不是 護士
Tā shì bú shì hùshi?
Is she a nurse or not?

5. How to Answer: 是 / 不是

When answering questions with 是, simply use 是 (shì) for "Yes" or 不是 (bú shì) for "No".

是 (Yes)
/
不是 (No)

You can also repeat the verb phrase or provide a complete sentence for clarity.

是, 我 是
Shì, wǒ shì.
Yes, I am.
