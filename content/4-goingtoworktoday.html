Going to Work Today

David:
你每天都來學校嗎？ (Nǐ měitiān dōu lái xuéxiào ma?) Do you come to school every day?
Amy:
對，我每天都來國小教書。 (Duì, wǒ měitiān dōu lái guóxiǎo jiāoshū.) Yes, I come to elementary school to teach every day.
David:
你教什麼？ (Nǐ jiāo shénme?) What do you teach?
Amy:
我教英文。 (Wǒ jiāo Yīngwén.) I teach English.
David:
今天累不累？ (Jīntiān lèi bu lèi?) Are you tired today?
Amy:
有一點累。 (Yǒu yìdiǎn lèi.) A little tired.
David:
你的班有多少學生？ (Nǐ de bān yǒu duōshǎo xuéshēng?) How many students are in your class?
Amy:
我的班有很多學生。 (Wǒ de bān yǒu hěn duō xuéshēng.) My class has many students.
David:
你常常跟學生說英文嗎？ (Nǐ chángcháng gēn xuéshēng shuō Yīngwén ma?) Do you often speak English with the students?
Amy:
對，我每天跟學生說英文，也教他們英文。 (Duì, wǒ měitiān gēn xuéshēng shuō Yīngwén, yě jiāo tāmen Yīngwén.) Yes, I speak English with the students every day and also teach them English.

Grammar & Notes

1. Time expression + Subject + Verb + Object

Words like 每天 (měitiān) = every day and 今天 (jīntiān) = today can be placed at the beginning of the sentence.

Pattern: Time + S + Verb + Object

每天我來學校。
Měitiān wǒ lái xuéxiào.
→ I come to school every day.

You can also put the time expression after the subject:

Pattern: S + Time + Verb + Object

我每天來學校。
Wǒ měitiān lái xuéxiào.
→ I come to school every day.

Both are correct.

2. Verb + 不 + Verb？ — “... or not?”

This is called an A-not-A question. It is used to ask whether someone does something or not.

Pattern: Verb + 不 + Verb？

今天累不累？
Jīntiān lèi bu lèi?
→ Are you tired today?

你教不教英文？
Nǐ jiāo bu jiāo Yīngwén?
→ Do you teach English or not?

你吃不吃飯？
Nǐ chī bu chī fàn?
→ Are you eating or not?

You can answer:

累。 → Yes, I’m tired.
不累。 → No, I’m not tired.

3. 有一點 + Adjective — “a little / somewhat...”

有一點 (yǒu yìdiǎn) means “a little” or “somewhat.”

Pattern: 有一點 + Adjective

有一點累。
Yǒu yìdiǎn lèi.
→ I’m a little tired.

有一點忙。
Yǒu yìdiǎn máng.
→ I’m a little busy.

有一點冷。
Yǒu yìdiǎn lěng.
→ It’s a little cold.

From your dialogue:

A: 今天累不累？ → Are you tired today?
B: 有一點累。 → I’m a little tired.

4. 跟 (gēn) — “with”

跟 means “with” when you do something with another person.

Pattern: Subject + 跟 + Person + Verb + Object

Example from your dialogue:

我每天跟學生說英文。
Wǒ měitiān gēn xuéshēng shuō Yīngwén.
→ I speak English with my students every day.

Breakdown:

我 = I
每天 = every day
跟學生 = with my students
說英文 = speak English

More examples:

我跟朋友吃飯。
Wǒ gēn péngyǒu chīfàn.
→ I eat with my friend.

我跟老師說話。
Wǒ gēn lǎoshī shuōhuà.
→ I talk with the teacher.

我跟媽媽喝咖啡。
Wǒ gēn māma hē kāfēi.
→ I drink coffee with my mom.

Easy way to remember: 跟 + Person + Verb = do something with someone
