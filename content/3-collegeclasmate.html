College Classmate (大學同學）

David :
Luna :
David：
你們以前是大學同學嗎？ Nǐmen yǐqián shì dàxué tóngxué ma? Were you university classmates?
Luna：
對，我們大學畢業以後，他在銀行工作，她在公司工作。 Duì, wǒmen dàxué bìyè yǐhòu, tā zài yínháng gōngzuò, tā zài gōngsī gōngzuò. Yes. After we graduated from university, he started working at a bank, and she started working at a company.
David：
她結婚了嗎？有孩子嗎？ Tā jiéhūn le ma? Yǒu háizi ma? Is she married? Does she have children?
Luna：
結婚了。她有一個兒子和一個女兒，他們都是很可愛的小朋友。 Jiéhūn le. Tā yǒu yí ge érzi hé yí ge nǚ'ér, tāmen dōu shì hěn kě'ài de xiǎopéngyǒu. Yes, she is married. She has a son and a daughter. They are both very cute children.
David：
那他呢？ Nà tā ne? What about him?
Luna：
他在學校工作，也常常用電腦讀書。 Tā zài xuéxiào gōngzuò, yě chángcháng yòng diànnǎo dúshū. He works at a school and often studies using a computer.
David：
你們現在都很忙嗎？ Nǐmen xiànzài dōu hěn máng ma? Are you all very busy now?
Luna：
對啊！工作很忙，但是很開心。 Duì a! Gōngzuò hěn máng, dànshì hěn kāixīn. Yes! Work is very busy, but I’m very happy.

Grammar & Notes

1. 在 — at / in

在 + place + verb is used to say where someone does something or works.

他在學校工作。
Tā zài xuéxiào gōngzuò.
He works at a school.
她在公司工作。
Tā zài gōngsī gōngzuò.
She works at a company.

Pattern:

Subject + 在 + Place + Verb

2. 了 — completed action / new situation

了 can be placed after a verb to indicate that an action has happened or been completed.

她結婚了。
Tā jiéhūn le.
She got married.

It can also indicate a change of situation.

他們大學畢業了。
Tāmen dàxué bìyè le.
They have graduated from university.

Note: 了 does not always simply mean “past tense.” Its meaning depends on the context.

3. 都 — all / both

都 is used when talking about two or more people or things and means “all” or “both.”

他們都是很可愛的小朋友。
Tāmen dōu shì hěn kě'ài de xiǎopéngyǒu.
They are both very cute children.
你們都很忙嗎？
Nǐmen dōu hěn máng ma?
Are you all very busy?

Pattern:

Subject + 都 + Verb / Adjective

4. 但是 — but / however

但是 connects two ideas that contrast with each other.

工作很忙，但是很開心。
Gōngzuò hěn máng, dànshì hěn kāixīn.
Work is very busy, but I’m very happy.

Pattern:

Idea 1 + 但是 + Idea 2
我很忙，但是我很開心。
Wǒ hěn máng, dànshì wǒ hěn kāixīn.
I’m very busy, but I’m happy.

5. 很 — very

很 is commonly placed before an adjective.

Subject + 很 + Adjective
他們很可愛。 / 工作很忙。
They are very cute. / Work is very busy.

In Mandarin, 很 + adjective is also a very common way to make a natural descriptive sentence. It does not always need to be translated literally as “very.”

6. 常常 — often

常常 means “often” and usually comes before the verb.

他也常常用電腦讀書。
Tā yě chángcháng yòng diànnǎo dúshū.
He also often studies using a computer.

Pattern:

Subject + 常常 + Verb
我常常讀書。
Wǒ chángcháng dúshū.
I often study.
